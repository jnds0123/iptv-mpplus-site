WHAT IS IN HERE

  Hospitality Solutions Deck.pptx
      The 25-slide pitch, for hotel owners and operators. Every
      slide carries speaker notes — open the Notes pane before
      presenting, the argument for each slide is written there.

  Hospitality Solutions Deck.pdf
      The same deck, for sending ahead of a meeting.

  Platform Architecture.pdf
      The technical specification. Ten modules, the data model,
      the Android device strategy, PBX integration, and the
      phased build sequence.

  Overview.pdf
      Short summary of the whole package.

  Demo/index.html
      A working prototype. Double-click to open in any browser —
      no install, no internet needed.

  Source/
      The editable originals. build_deck.js generates the
      PowerPoint; edit that rather than the .pptx so the deck
      can be rebuilt cleanly.

RUNNING THE DEMO

  Open Demo/index.html. Three tabs across the top:

    Guest television   Built in the Orpheus Nations interface —
                       the same rail, featured cards and poster
                       rows as the website. Drive it with the
                       arrow keys, Enter and Backspace, the four
                       buttons a hotel remote has. Entertainment
                       sits above the divider in the menu, your
                       stay below it: order dinner from Dining,
                       book the spa from Reservations.

    Pre-arrival        The guest's own phone. Scan the passport,
                       check the parsed details, sign with the
                       mouse.

    Hotel console      Everything the staff see.

  The point is the cross-link. Do something on a guest screen,
  then open the Hotel console: the kitchen order, the service
  ticket, the folio line and the signed documents are already
  there, because one guest record sits behind every screen.
  That is the argument the whole deck is making.

BEFORE THIS GOES TO A CLIENT

  Four things are deliberately marked rather than invented.

  1. Orpheus Nations content. The catalogue, channel line-up
     and licensed territories on the entertainment slide are
     placeholders. The site was not reachable when this was
     built.

  2. The commercial model. The chart is relative and
     illustrative — no client figures are in it. Rebuild it on
     the group's own outlet revenue, vendor spend, payroll and
     capex plan.

  3. Certification. SOC 2 and the penetration test are stated
     as commitments with a date, NOT as credentials already
     held. Correct anyone who reads it the other way.

  4. PBX and PMS integration is scoped per vendor. The deck
     deliberately promises no generic connector.

  Two constraints worth raising yourself before a client's IT
  raises them:

  - Samsung Tizen and LG webOS televisions cannot run the
    Android app natively. Those properties need a small Android
    device behind the set until the televisions refresh.

  - "No set-top box" holds on hospitality-model Android
    televisions, which permit managed install and lockdown.
    Consumer models generally do not.
